# Functional audit, September 2026

Asked for as "there is no way to delete a teacher — and find anything else like
that: a function that is missing, broken, or too hard to do."

## How it was checked

Guessing screen by screen would miss things, so each check started from the
schema and the code:

1. **Every table in `public`, against what the app can do to it.** For each of
   the 118 tables: does the app create, edit or delete it, directly or through a
   function (239 RPCs are called from `src/`)? Where a table has a delete policy
   but no screen, or a screen but a dangerous delete, it was probed.
2. **What deleting would actually remove.** Every foreign key pointing at
   `students`, `staff`, `people`, `sections` and `class_levels`, with its
   `ON DELETE` action. Then a delete was performed as an administrator, in a
   rolled-back transaction.
3. **A newly signed-up college, from nothing.** Northgate Academy was compared
   with the demo college.
4. **The sign-in lifecycle.** Invite, sign in, leave, forget a password.

Every number below was measured on the live database. Every probe was rolled
back.

## Fixed in this change (migration `0288`)

### 1. A teacher could not be deleted, nor a student

**Finding.** There was *Record leaving* but no delete anywhere, for staff or for
students. A duplicate entry, or somebody added to the wrong college, stayed
for ever.

**Fixed.** A **Delete** button now sits on the staff record and the student
record, for anybody with `staff.manage` / `students.manage`. It removes a record
that has no history, together with:

- the person row, when they are nobody else here;
- any guardian of a deleted student who is left with no child, no login and no
  invitation.

A record with history is refused with a sentence naming what is held, and the
dialog points to *Record leaving*. For example:

> *Ishaan Chauhan cannot be deleted: the school holds 4 fee ledger entries,
> 1 invoice, 20 attendance marks, 8 exam marks, 1 exam result, 1 library loan,
> 1 homework submission, 1 hostel booking and 1 bus seat for them.*

### 2. Critical: one delete could erase a family's fee account

**Finding.** The administrator policies on `students`, `staff`, `sections` and
`class_levels` allow DELETE. Almost every table recording a person's history
hangs off them `ON DELETE CASCADE`. Measured as an administrator, rolled back:

| deleted | what went with it |
|---|---|
| one student | 4 fee ledger entries, 1 invoice |
| one teacher | 3 payslips, 51 attendance marks |

Rule 6 makes the fee ledger append-only by revoking UPDATE and DELETE. But
a cascade runs as the table owner, so that revoke does not stop it. No screen
issued this delete, but a single PostgREST call from a signed-in administrator
could.

**Fixed.** `BEFORE DELETE` triggers on all four tables refuse while history
exists:

- **Students:** fees, invoices, register, marks, results, certificates, library,
  submitted homework, store purchases, online tests, leave, bus, hostel,
  concessions, logins and pending invitations.
- **Staff:** payslips, register, leave, certificates, cover, library, store
  issues, biometric punches, logins and invitations.
- **Sections:** any child ever enrolled, or any syllabus record.
- **Classes:** any section in any year.

Deleting a whole college is exempt.

### 3. Fourteen foreign keys could never do their job

**Finding.** Fourteen foreign keys were written as
`(tenant_id, x) … ON DELETE SET NULL`. That nulls `tenant_id` as well as `x`,
and `tenant_id` is a NOT NULL column, so deleting the parent always failed with
a constraint error. Found when deleting an empty section failed on
`import_rows`.

**Fixed.** Each now reads `SET NULL (x)`. The tables affected:

- certificates
- enquiries (×3)
- hostels
- import_rows (×2)
- inventory_items
- stock_movements
- transport_routes
- vehicles (×2)
- visitors (×2)

### 4. Blocker: a new college could not create a single class

**Finding.** No screen anywhere creates or edits classes or sections. The
first-run checklist told a new college to *"Create this year's classes"* and
linked to `/academics`, which had no such control. Northgate Academy, a college
that signed up, has **0 classes and 0 sections**. That leaves nowhere to admit a
child, take a register or set fees. The demo college only has classes because
the seed made them.

**Fixed.** **Academics → Classes** is now the first tab, and opens by default
while a college has no classes. It can:

- add, rename and reorder classes;
- add, edit and delete this year's sections, with seats and class teacher;
- show how many children are in each section.

Deleting is refused while children are in a section, or while a class still has
sections. The checklist now links straight to that tab.

## Still open, in order of importance

### High

**H1. A teacher who leaves can still sign in and read everything.**
`staff_exit` ends their lessons, class-teacher duty and library card, but does
not touch their login. No policy consults `staff.status`; they compare the role
held in the sign-in token. So a departed teacher's account reads what any
teacher reads, which was measured at **302 of 302** students (rule 4, `0260`).
This was established by reading the policies, not by signing in as a leaver.

Nothing in the product lists who can sign in:

- Settings → Team shows invitations only, not accepted logins.
- There is no way to change a login's role.
- There is no way to remove a login's access.

*Needs:* a "who can sign in" list, with remove access (block the auth user,
strip the tenant from its metadata, end its sessions) and change role.
`staff_exit` should call it.

**H2. No "Forgot password", no "Change password".**
There is no call to `resetPasswordForEmail` or `updateUser` anywhere, and no
auth callback route. A parent who forgets their password has no way back in
short of an administrator re-inviting them to a new address.

*Needs:* a *Forgot password* link on the sign-in page, a reset page, and
*Change password* in the account menu.

**H3. Four other deletes are guarded only by the button.**

- **Exams.** An exam's Delete button is disabled once the exam is published, but
  the database accepts the delete. It cascades into marks and published
  results.
- **Homework.** Deleting homework removes graded submissions after a single
  browser `confirm()`.
- **Periods.** Deleting a period removes every lesson in that period, all
  week, in every class, together with their cover arrangements. There is no
  count of what goes.
- **Books.** Deleting a book removes its lending history. When a fine was
  charged on it, the delete fails with a raw foreign-key error instead.

*Needs:* the same shape as `0288`: refuse in the database while the thing
has history, and say what.

### Medium

**M1. Fee heads can be added but never edited or switched off.**
`saveFeeHead` accepts an id, but no screen calls it with one. The page itself
says *"mark it inactive instead"*, and there is no control to do that.

**M2. Giving a teacher a login takes a second screen.**
After adding a teacher, the office goes to Settings → Team, picks the role and
searches for the same person again. The staff record has no *Invite to sign in*
button. The student record's guardian card does the equivalent for families.

**M3. No bulk import for staff.**
Students have a spreadsheet import with preview. A college with sixty staff
types each one in.

**M4. The library cannot name categories or retire members.**
Books take an optional category, but nothing can create a category, so every
book has none. Library members can be added but never suspended or removed. A
card only closes through a formal leaving.

**M5. Store item categories can be added but not renamed or removed.**

### Low

**L1. Several delete actions show the raw database message.**
`deleteExam`, `deletePaper`, `deleteTimeSlot`, `deleteClassRoom`,
`deleteHomework`, `deleteFeeStructure` and `deleteBook` pass `error.message`
straight to the toast. `deleteSubject`, `deleteRoute` and `deleteStop` already
translate `23503` into a sentence, and are the pattern to copy.

**L2. A child admitted by mistake with the admission fee switched on cannot be
deleted.**
Their invoice has a gapless number, and a hole in that series is one nobody
can audit. So they are recorded as having left, with the reason. This is
deliberate, and written down here so it is not reported as a bug.

## What was checked and found sound

- Every other table with a delete in the app refuses or cascades only into its
  own drafts.
- Transport, hostel and stock items have active/inactive switches in place of
  delete.
- Invitations can be revoked. Guardians can be unlinked. Concessions can be
  revoked. Invoices can be cancelled, and ledger entries reversed rather than
  deleted.
- All five database guards read 0 after `0288`.

## Cost

Built before and after with `npm run build`:

- `/academics`: 177 → 180 kB, for the Classes tab.
- `/staff/[id]`: 168 → 169 kB, for the Delete dialog.
- Every other route and the 103 kB shared bundle are unchanged, including
  `/students/[id]`.

`tests/people/a-record-with-history-is-kept.test.ts` pins the triggers, their
definer shape and exemption, the delete order, the photograph order, the
server-side year on a new section, and that no later migration writes a
composite `SET NULL` again. It was checked by planting three violations: a
composite `SET NULL`, an invoker guard that refuses nothing, and a photograph
removed before the row. Each was caught.
