# Functional audit, September 2026

Asked for as "there is no way to delete a teacher — and find anything else like
that: a function that is missing, broken, or too hard to do."

## How it was checked

Guessing screen by screen would miss things, so each check started from the
schema and the code:

1. **Every table in `public`, against what the app can do to it.** For each of
   the 118 tables: does the app create, edit or delete it, directly or through a
   function (239 RPCs are called from `src/`)? Where a table has a delete policy
   but no screen, or a screen but a dangerous delete, it was probed.
2. **What deleting would actually remove.** Every foreign key pointing at
   `students`, `staff`, `people`, `sections` and `class_levels`, with its
   `ON DELETE` action. Then a delete was performed as an administrator, in a
   rolled-back transaction.
3. **A newly signed-up college, from nothing.** Northgate Academy was compared
   with the demo college.
4. **The sign-in lifecycle.** Invite, sign in, leave, forget a password.

Every number below was measured on the live database. Every probe was rolled
back.

## Fixed in this change (migration `0288`)

### 1. A teacher could not be deleted, nor a student

**Finding.** There was *Record leaving* but no delete anywhere, for staff or for
students. A duplicate entry, or somebody added to the wrong college, stayed
for ever.

**Fixed.** A **Delete** button now sits on the staff record and the student
record, for anybody with `staff.manage` / `students.manage`. It removes a record
that has no history, together with:

- the person row, when they are nobody else here;
- any guardian of a deleted student who is left with no child, no login and no
  invitation.

A record with history is refused with a sentence naming what is held, and the
dialog points to *Record leaving*. For example:

> *Ishaan Chauhan cannot be deleted: the school holds 4 fee ledger entries,
> 1 invoice, 20 attendance marks, 8 exam marks, 1 exam result, 1 library loan,
> 1 homework submission, 1 hostel booking and 1 bus seat for them.*

### 2. Critical: one delete could erase a family's fee account

**Finding.** The administrator policies on `students`, `staff`, `sections` and
`class_levels` allow DELETE. Almost every table recording a person's history
hangs off them `ON DELETE CASCADE`. Measured as an administrator, rolled back:

| deleted | what went with it |
|---|---|
| one student | 4 fee ledger entries, 1 invoice |
| one teacher | 3 payslips, 51 attendance marks |

Rule 6 makes the fee ledger append-only by revoking UPDATE and DELETE. But
a cascade runs as the table owner, so that revoke does not stop it. No screen
issued this delete, but a single PostgREST call from a signed-in administrator
could.

**Fixed.** `BEFORE DELETE` triggers on all four tables refuse while history
exists:

- **Students:** fees, invoices, register, marks, results, certificates, library,
  submitted homework, store purchases, online tests, leave, bus, hostel,
  concessions, logins and pending invitations.
- **Staff:** payslips, register, leave, certificates, cover, library, store
  issues, biometric punches, logins and invitations.
- **Sections:** any child ever enrolled, or any syllabus record.
- **Classes:** any section in any year.

Deleting a whole college is exempt.

### 3. Fourteen foreign keys could never do their job

**Finding.** Fourteen foreign keys were written as
`(tenant_id, x) … ON DELETE SET NULL`. That nulls `tenant_id` as well as `x`,
and `tenant_id` is a NOT NULL column, so deleting the parent always failed with
a constraint error. Found when deleting an empty section failed on
`import_rows`.

**Fixed.** Each now reads `SET NULL (x)`. The tables affected:

- certificates
- enquiries (×3)
- hostels
- import_rows (×2)
- inventory_items
- stock_movements
- transport_routes
- vehicles (×2)
- visitors (×2)

### 4. Blocker: a new college could not create a single class

**Finding.** No screen anywhere creates or edits classes or sections. The
first-run checklist told a new college to *"Create this year's classes"* and
linked to `/academics`, which had no such control. Northgate Academy, a college
that signed up, has **0 classes and 0 sections**. That leaves nowhere to admit a
child, take a register or set fees. The demo college only has classes because
the seed made them.

**Fixed.** **Academics → Classes** is now the first tab, and opens by default
while a college has no classes. It can:

- add, rename and reorder classes;
- add, edit and delete this year's sections, with seats and class teacher;
- show how many children are in each section.

Deleting is refused while children are in a section, or while a class still has
sections. The checklist now links straight to that tab.

## Fixed in the follow-up (migration `0289`)

Everything that was left open after `0288`, except L2, which is deliberate.

### H1. A teacher who left could still sign in

**Fixed.** *Settings → Team* now begins with **Who can sign in**: every login,
with its role, the record it acts as, when it last signed in, and whether it can
sign in now. From the list an administrator can:

- **change a role.** Only roles the login's record can hold are offered. A
  teacher cannot be made a Parent, because a Parent login has to name a
  guardian.
- **switch a login off or on.** Switching off does three things. It marks the
  profile inactive. It bans the auth user. It ends the user's sessions.
  Nothing of theirs is deleted.

Recording that a member of staff has left now switches their logins off
automatically, through a trigger on `staff.status`. Switching one back on is
always a decision made on this screen.

Two refusals keep the college manageable:

- nobody can change or switch off their own login;
- the last active login holding `users.manage` cannot be removed.

A leaver who is that last login is left open, with a notice.

**The limit, stated.** Ending the sessions stops the refresh token. An access
token that was already issued stays valid until it expires, at most an hour,
for a direct API call. The app itself signs them out on the next page, because
the middleware asks Supabase about the user on every request.

Probed as an administrator, rolled back:

| action | result |
|---|---|
| switch off the teacher | banned, inactive |
| switch the teacher back on | can sign in again |
| teacher → Accountant | the token role reads `accountant` |
| teacher → Parent | refused with a sentence |
| switch off your own login | refused |
| teacher records a resignation | the login closes with it |
| a teacher asks for the list | refused |

### H2. No way back from a forgotten password

**Fixed.** The flow:

1. *Forgot your password?* on the sign-in page leads to `/auth/forgot`.
2. That page sends a reset link. It always answers "if that address has a
   login, a link is on its way", so the page cannot be used to find out which
   addresses have logins.
3. The link lands on `/auth/callback`, which accepts only an in-app `next`.
4. The person sets a new password on `/auth/reset`.

*Change password* is in the account menu and asks for the current password
first. A switched-off login trying to sign in is told it was switched off,
rather than "wrong password".

**One setting outside the code.** Supabase only sends a link to an address on
its list. Add `https://<your domain>/auth/callback` under Authentication → URL
Configuration → Redirect URLs, or the reset email links to the Site URL
instead.

### H3. Four more deletes guarded only by the button

**Fixed** with `BEFORE DELETE` definer triggers, the `0288` shape:

- **Exams.** A published exam is refused, and so is a paper of a published
  exam.
- **Homework.** Homework with marked submissions is refused. Probed: 13 marked
  submissions, refused.
- **Periods.** A period is refused while a cover arrangement hangs off one of
  its lessons. The confirmation now also says how many lessons go with the
  period. The demo college has no cover arrangements, so this refusal was read
  and not exercised.
- **Books.** A book that has ever been lent is refused. Probed: "Atlas of the
  World", lent 2 times, refused.

Homework's attachments are now removed only *after* the row is deleted.
Removing them first would have emptied a refused homework's files.

### M1. Fee heads could not be edited or switched off

**Fixed.** A pencil on each fee head opens it for editing, with an *In use*
switch.

### M2. Giving a teacher a login took a second screen

**Fixed.** The staff record offers *Give a login* while the person is current,
has no login and has no pending invitation. It offers only staff roles.

### M3. No bulk import for staff

**Fixed.** `/staff/import` reads a CSV, or text pasted from a spreadsheet. It
shows every row as editable cells, with what is wrong beside each row:

- missing required columns;
- an employee code used twice in the file;
- a date that is not a date;
- an unknown gender;
- a bad email.

Every row is re-judged after any edit. *Add* writes the ready rows through
`staff_admit`, the same function the *Add staff* form uses. A row the database
refuses stays on screen with its reason. It takes at most 200 rows, and a
longer file is refused rather than cut short.

Building it found a bug in the student importer's shared date parser. It
accepted `2024-13-40`, which then failed later as a raw Postgres error. It also
accepted `31/02/2024`. Both are refused now.

### M4. The library could not name categories or manage members

**Correction to this audit.** It said members "can be added". They could not.
`createMember` existed and nothing called it.

**Fixed.**

- *Library → Members* has **Add member**, for a student or a member of staff.
- Each card can be suspended and reinstated.
- *Library → Books* has **Categories**: add, rename, delete. A category in use
  cannot be deleted.

### M5. Store item categories could not be renamed or removed

**Fixed** with the same Categories dialog. It is one component, taking each
module's own actions as props (rule 8).

### L1. Delete actions showed the raw database message

**Fixed** in all seven actions:

- `deleteExam`, `deletePaper`
- `deleteTimeSlot`, `deleteClassRoom`
- `deleteHomework`
- `deleteFeeStructure`
- `deleteBook`

`src/lib/validations/errors.ts` turns the error into a sentence:

- a guard's own sentence passes through unchanged;
- a foreign key names the kind of record still pointing at the row;
- a refusal names the role.

Each delete now also counts what it removed. A delete that no policy matches
raises nothing (rule 6), so it used to report success. It now says *Nothing
was deleted*.

### Still open, deliberately

**L2. A child admitted by mistake with the admission fee switched on cannot be
deleted.**
Their invoice has a gapless number, and a hole in that series is one nobody
can audit. So they are recorded as having left, with the reason. This is
deliberate, and written down here so it is not reported as a bug.

## What was checked and found sound

- Every other table with a delete in the app refuses or cascades only into its
  own drafts.
- Transport, hostel and stock items have active/inactive switches in place of
  delete.
- Invitations can be revoked. Guardians can be unlinked. Concessions can be
  revoked. Invoices can be cancelled, and ledger entries reversed rather than
  deleted.
- All five database guards read 0 after `0288`, and again after `0289`.

## Cost

Built before and after with `npm run build`:

- `/academics`: 177 → 180 kB, for the Classes tab.
- `/staff/[id]`: 168 → 169 kB, for the Delete dialog.
- Every other route and the 103 kB shared bundle are unchanged, including
  `/students/[id]`.

`tests/people/a-record-with-history-is-kept.test.ts` pins the triggers, their
definer shape and exemption, the delete order, the photograph order, the
server-side year on a new section, and that no later migration writes a
composite `SET NULL` again. It was checked by planting three violations: a
composite `SET NULL`, an invoker guard that refuses nothing, and a photograph
removed before the row. Each was caught.

### Cost of `0289`

Built before and after:

- The shared bundle is unchanged at 103 kB.
- New routes: `/auth/forgot` and `/auth/reset` 122 kB, `/account` 127 kB,
  `/staff/import` 133 kB.

  trigger.
- `/login` and `/start` +3 kB, for the new sign-in strings.
- `/settings/team` 132 → 133 kB and `/library/books` 188 → 189 kB.

The first build said otherwise:

- `/settings/team` +34 kB. The role picker used Radix Select, one per row. It is
  a native `<select>` now.
- `/inventory` 135 → 157 kB and `/library/books` +13 kB. The Categories dialog
  was imported statically. Now only its button is; the dialog loads on the
  click that opens it.
- `/library/members` +21 kB, for the same reason with Add member.

`tests/auth/who-can-sign-in.test.ts` pins:

- the login functions' definer shape, gate, tenant filter and revokes;
- the three writes of a switch-off, and the leaver trigger;
- the five new delete guards;
- homework's file order;
- the seven translated deletes;
- the reset and change-password flow;

It was checked by planting four violations, and each was caught:

- a raw `error.message` in `deleteBook`;
- a later `team_logins` with no tenant filter;

- a `login_close` that only flips the profile flag.
